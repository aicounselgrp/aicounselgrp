Autonomous agents that can take real-world actions — placing orders, sending
communications, modifying records — raise a question courts haven't settled:
when the agent acts outside its intended scope, whose duty of care was
breached?

For counsel advising deployers, three threads are worth tracking:

1. **Delegation vs. negligent supervision.** Courts are borrowing heavily from
   agency law, but agentic AI doesn't fit cleanly into "employee" or
   "independent contractor" categories.
2. **Contractual allocation.** Vendor agreements increasingly try to shift
   liability downstream through indemnification and limitation-of-liability
   clauses — but regulators in several jurisdictions are starting to push back
   on enforceability where consumer harm is involved.
3. **Insurance gaps.** Many E&O and tech liability policies were written
   before agentic deployments were common, and exclusions can be broader than
   clients expect.

This is exactly the kind of cross-cutting issue this group exists to track
collectively rather than everyone re-deriving it in isolation.
